Google Ads vs Meta Ads: Which is Better for Your Business in 2026?

📅 2026-02-01✍ AdsOnUs Team📖 5 min read

The most common question we get from new clients: "Should I run Google Ads or Facebook Ads?" The honest answer is that it depends on your business — and often, the right answer is both. But let's break down the key differences.

The Fundamental Difference: Intent vs Interest

Google Ads targets intent. When someone searches "buy running shoes near me," they're actively looking for what you sell. You intercept them at the moment of maximum purchase intent. This is why Google Search has consistently high conversion rates — you're showing up for people who already want what you're offering.

Meta Ads targets interest. You're interrupting someone's scroll to show them something they didn't know they needed (yet). This requires more compelling creative and a longer consideration period for most product categories — but it lets you reach people before they even know they're in-market.

When Google Ads Wins

Google Search excels when: people actively search for your product or service category, your business solves a specific problem people know they have, your market has existing demand, and you can capture high-intent buyers at the bottom of the funnel. Real estate agencies, restaurants, dentists, repair services, and B2B software all tend to perform strongly on Google Search.

When Meta Ads Wins

Meta excels when: you're building demand for a product people don't know exists yet, you can show compelling visuals (fashion, food, beauty), you want to reach a specific demographic segment, or you sell impulse purchases with broad appeal. Fashion boutiques, beauty salons, bakeries, gyms, and event-based businesses often see better ROI on Meta.

Cost Comparison

Google Search typically has higher CPCs (cost per click) than Meta because you're bidding on specific intent keywords. A keyword like "real estate agency Baku" might cost $2–$8 per click. On Meta, you might reach people for $0.20–$1.00 per click — but lower click quality means lower conversion rates. In practice, both platforms can achieve similar cost per acquisition when managed correctly.

Creative Requirements

Google Search ads are text-only — your headline and description need to be sharp and relevant. Meta ads are visual-first — poor creative will kill your campaign regardless of how good your targeting is. This means Meta requires more upfront creative investment but gives you richer storytelling opportunities.

The Most Common Answer: Both

For most businesses beyond a minimum scale, running both platforms is more efficient than choosing one. Use Google to capture bottom-of-funnel intent. Use Meta to build awareness, retarget website visitors, and reach audiences who would never think to search for you. The two platforms feed each other — Meta creates the demand, Google captures it.

If budget forces you to choose one: start with Google Search if you're in a service business with clear demand (dentist, accountant, contractor). Start with Meta if you're in a product business where visual appeal drives the decision (fashion, food, beauty).
