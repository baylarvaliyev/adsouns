How Much Should Your Business Spend on Advertising? A Practical Framework

📅 2026-03-01✍ AdsOnUs Team📖 5 min read

One of the first questions every business asks is "how much should I spend on ads?" There is no single right answer, but there are frameworks that help you calculate a budget that makes sense for your specific situation.

Start With Your Target Cost Per Acquisition

The most important number in any ad budget discussion is your Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) target — how much you can afford to spend to acquire one customer while remaining profitable. To calculate this: take your average order value, subtract your cost of goods and fulfillment, then decide what percentage of that margin you're willing to invest in customer acquisition.

Example: If you sell products with an average order value of $80 and your cost of goods is $30, your gross margin is $50. If you're willing to spend 40% of that margin on acquisition, your target CAC is $20.

The Industry Benchmarks

Most industries allocate 5–12% of revenue to marketing and advertising. Early-stage businesses trying to grow quickly often spend 15–20% of revenue or more. Established businesses with strong word-of-mouth referrals sometimes spend as little as 2–5%. E-commerce typically runs at 8–15%. Service businesses (agencies, consultants) often run 10–20% because customer lifetime value is high.

The Platform Minimum

Each ad platform has an effective minimum budget below which the algorithm doesn't have enough data to optimize. For Meta Ads: $15–$30/day per ad set. For Google Search: $20–$50/day per campaign. For TikTok: $20–$50/day. For LinkedIn: $50–$100/day (the platform has a minimum bid that makes smaller budgets inefficient).

Running below these minimums is often worse than not running ads at all — you're spending money to collect insufficient data, making it impossible to make informed optimization decisions.

Starting Budget Recommendation

For a business new to paid advertising, we recommend starting with a "learning budget" that is explicitly set aside for data collection rather than immediate profitability. A minimum of $1,000–$2,000 over 6–8 weeks allows enough data for meaningful optimization decisions. Expect the first 2–3 weeks to be above your target CAC as the algorithm learns.

Scaling Budget

Once you find a profitable unit economics (your actual CAC is below your target CAC), the question becomes how fast to scale. A common rule: increase budget by no more than 20–30% per week, as larger increases can disrupt the learning phase and spike costs. Monitor CAC closely as you scale — it almost always rises as you exhaust your most responsive audience segments.

Ad Spend vs Management Fee

When working with an agency like AdsOnUs, your total advertising investment has two components: the ad spend (money paid directly to Meta, Google, TikTok) and the management fee (what you pay the agency). These are always separate. A common question: "Why should I pay a management fee when I'm already spending on ads?" The answer is that the management fee is an investment in extracting more value from your ad spend — through better targeting, creative, and optimization. An experienced team typically improves ROAS by 2–4× compared to self-managed accounts.
